# GT10BLDG-P6-Schedule
Primavera P6 construction schedule for a G+10 residential building: 140 activities, 176 relationships, 6-day calendar, floor-by-floor cycle, baseline, and Gantt reports (Oct 2026 – Feb 2028).
Project Description: Construction of G+10 Residential Building — Planning & Scheduling

Project Type: Residential High-Rise Construction (Ground + 10 Floors)
Role Simulated: Project Planning Engineer
Tool Used: Primavera P6 Professional (v25)
Built-up Area: Approx. 40,000 sq. ft.
Planned Duration: 01-Oct-2026 to 27-Sep-2027 (~12 months, 6-day working week)

Project Overview

This project involved developing a complete construction schedule for a Ground+10 residential building using Primavera P6, covering the full lifecycle from site mobilization through handover. The objective was to simulate the real planning workflow followed by planning engineers on live construction sites — translating a Bill of Quantities and construction methodology into a structured, time-phased, resource-loaded schedule capable of tracking progress and supporting decision-making throughout execution.

Scope of Work

The schedule was broken down into 7 major work packages (WBS Level 1) and 27 total WBS nodes, covering:

Preliminary & Mobilization — site survey, layout marking, labor and equipment mobilization, temporary facilities
Substructure — excavation, anti-termite treatment, PCC, footing reinforcement and concreting, backfilling, plinth beam construction
Superstructure — floor-by-floor RCC cycle (Ground Floor through Floor 10), each comprising column reinforcement, column concreting, slab shuttering, slab reinforcement, slab concreting, and de-shuttering
Masonry & Plastering — block work and internal plastering per floor, plus external plastering
MEP Works — electrical conduiting and plumbing rough-in per floor, HVAC ducting, and fire-fighting piping
Finishing Works — flooring, internal and external painting, doors and windows installation
Handover & Testing — MEP testing and commissioning, snagging, final cleaning, and formal handover

In total, the schedule comprises 140 individual activities, sequenced using Finish-to-Start (FS) and Start-to-Start (SS) relationships with appropriate lags to reflect real site constraints — most notably, a curing lag between footing concreting and backfilling, and staggered trade overlaps between structural and finishing work to compress the overall timeline.

Methodology
WBS-first approach: The Work Breakdown Structure was designed to mirror the actual construction sequence and billing structure used on site, making it directly usable for progress measurement and interim billing against a Bill of Quantities.
Duration-driven scheduling: Activity durations were estimated based on typical productivity rates for RCC and finishing trades on a G+10 structure, then linked through logical relationships so that Primavera P6 calculates all start and finish dates automatically — rather than manually entering dates, which reflects correct professional practice in scheduling software.
Floor-cycle repetition: Since superstructure work repeats a nearly identical 6-activity cycle across 11 levels (Ground Floor + 10 typical floors), the schedule demonstrates understanding of cyclical/repetitive activity planning — a core skill in high-rise construction planning, where identifying and optimizing the repeating cycle time directly drives overall project duration.
Critical path awareness: The superstructure phase, being the longest and most sequentially dependent portion of the project, forms the primary driver of overall project duration. Floor-to-floor dependencies were deliberately structured so that compressing the per-floor cycle time (e.g., through overlapping trades or additional resources) directly shortens the critical path — a key lever available to a planning engineer managing schedule performance.
Key Technical Elements Demonstrated
WBS creation and hierarchical activity organization in Primavera P6
Activity duration estimation and Finish-to-Start/Start-to-Start relationship logic with lags
Use of P6's native scheduling engine (F9) to calculate the network logic rather than manual date entry
Calendar configuration (6-day working week) reflecting realistic site conditions
Baseline creation for future Planned vs. Actual tracking and variance analysis
Resource categorization (labor, material, equipment) at the activity level for future cost and resource loading
Deliverables Produced
A structured Primavera P6 project file (WBS + 140 activities + partial logic network)
A master planning spreadsheet with full activity list, durations, WBS mapping, and relationship logic
Phase-level and activity-level Gantt chart visualizations
A supporting project plan summary document covering scope, schedule logic, and key discussion points for technical review
Relevance to the Role

This exercise mirrors the core responsibility of a planning/billing engineer at a construction organization — converting design and BOQ information into a structured, trackable schedule; understanding how repetitive high-rise construction cycles drive project duration; and being able to explain scheduling logic (why an activity is sequenced where it is) rather than simply producing a Gantt chart. It also reflects practical, hands-on experience navigating Primavera P6's WBS, Activities, Predecessors, and Scheduling modules — the same environment used industry-wide for EPC and construction project controls.
